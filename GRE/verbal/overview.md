# Verbal Reasoning -- Overview

The Verbal section is NOT a vocabulary-recall test alone -- it tests **reading comprehension and
reasoning** under time pressure. Three question types:

---

## 1. Reading Comprehension (RC)

Read a passage (1 to several paragraphs, academic prose) and answer questions.

**Sub-types:**
- **Multiple-choice, select one** (5 options).
- **Multiple-choice, select one or more** (no partial credit).
- **Select-in-passage** -- click the sentence in the passage that fits the description.

**Strategy:**
- Read actively for **main idea**, **author's tone**, and **structure** (the "why" of each paragraph).
- Answer from the **passage only**, not outside knowledge -- even if the science is wrong.
- Distinguish **stated facts** vs **inferences** vs **author's opinion**.
- Beware: correct answers are often **mild/qualified**; wrong answers often **overstate** (all/none/
  always/never) or bring in info not in the passage.
- For "primary purpose" / "main idea" questions, the answer should cover the **whole** passage.

**Pacing:** ~1.5-2 min per RC question including reading. Long passages -> skim structure first,
then read relevant parts per question.

---

## 2. Text Completion (TC)

A sentence (or 2-3 sentences) with **1, 2, or 3 blanks**. Pick the best word for each blank from
the given options. **No partial credit** -- all blanks must be correct.

**Strategy:**
- Read the full sentence; identify **clue words** (transitions: although, moreover, despite,
  therefore, ironically...) that signal the blank's direction (same vs opposite).
- **Predict** a word/idea BEFORE looking at the choices.
- For multi-blank: solve the blank with the strongest clue first.
- Plug all choices back in; the correct set must make the sentence coherent as a whole.
- Watch for sentences where the logic "turns" (a positive word followed by "however" flips meaning).

---

## 3. Sentence Equivalence (SE)

One sentence, **one blank**, **six choices**, pick **two** words. The two chosen words must both
(1) produce a sentence with the **same meaning** and (2) each fit grammatically. **No partial
credit** -- both must be right.

**Strategy:**
- Predict a meaning BEFORE scanning choices.
- Look for a **pair** of synonyms among the six -- but be careful: synonyms that don't both fit the
  sentence's logic are a trap. The pair must keep the sentence's meaning identical.
- If you can't find a synonym pair, re-read for the clue you missed.
- Eliminate words with no plausible partner first.

---

## General verbal strategy

- **Predict before you peek** at choices -- this defeats the "all options look plausible" trap.
- **Vocabulary is the enabler** for TC/SE -- see `vocabulary.md`. Daily review is non-negotiable.
- **RC is the enabler** for the hardest points -- practice reading dense academic text fast.
- Pacing: ~1 min per TC/SE, ~1.5-2 min per RC. Skip-and-mark anything over ~90 seconds.
