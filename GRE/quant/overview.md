# Quantitative Reasoning -- Overview

The Quant section tests **basic math reasoning**, not advanced math. Concepts top out at early
high-school level (no calculus/trig). The challenge is **speed, careful reading, and avoiding traps**.

---

## Math content areas (ETS Math Review structure)

1. **Arithmetic** -- properties of integers, divisibility/primes/factorization, exponents & roots,
   decimals, percents, ratios, absolute value, number lines.
2. **Algebra** -- operations with algebraic expressions, factoring, linear & quadratic equations,
   inequalities, functions & "symbolism", coordinate geometry (lines, slopes).
3. **Geometry** -- parallel/perpendicular lines, circles, triangles (incl. special + Pythagorean),
   quadrilaterals, polygons, area/perimeter/volume, angles. (No formal proofs needed.)
4. **Data Analysis** -- basic descriptive stats (mean/median/mode/range/SD), interpretation of
   tables/graphs/charts, elementary probability, counting (permutations/combinations), Venn diagrams.

> Refresh these with the free **ETS Math Review PDF**: https://globalhighered.ets.org/gre-math-review

## Question types (4 formats)

### 1. Quantitative Comparison (QC)
Compare two quantities, **Quantity A** vs **Quantity B**. Answer choices are always:
- (A) Quantity A is greater
- (B) Quantity B is greater
- (C) The two quantities are equal
- (D) The relationship cannot be determined from the information given

**Strategy:** simplify, plug numbers (try 0, 1, negatives, fractions), avoid assuming variables are
integers/positive unless stated. (D) is correct only when you can produce two different outcomes.

### 2. Multiple Choice -- Select One
Classic 5-option single-answer. **Strategy:** backsolve from the choices; estimate.

### 3. Multiple Choice -- Select One or More
Variable number of correct options (no partial credit). **Strategy:** treat each option as a
true/false statement; check all.

### 4. Numeric Entry
Type the answer (integer, fraction, or decimal). **Strategy:** no guessing; double-check units and
format; verify by plugging back in.

### Data Interpretation (sets)
Several questions referring to a graph/table. Read the title, units, and legend carefully before
calculating. These reward accuracy over speed.

## Calculator strategy

- The on-screen calculator is **basic** (+, -, *, /, sqrt). No parentheses/PEMDAS, slow input.
- **Default to mental/paper math.** Use the calculator mainly for ugly decimals/large products.
- Beware: the calculator does **not** respect order of operations (it computes left-to-right with
  no grouping) and won't estimate -- so use estimation first.

---

## Common traps & pitfalls

- **"Number" vs "integer"** -- numbers can be fractions/negatives; integers cannot.
- **Assuming positivity** -- variables may be 0 or negative unless stated.
- **Even exponents hide the sign** -- x^2 = 9 -> x = 3 OR -3.
- **Percent of vs percent off** -- "100 increased by 10%" = 110, not "10% of 100".
- **Figures not drawn to scale** -- never eyeball geometry; derive from given facts.
- **Units** -- watch min vs hr, $ vs cents, diameter vs radius.
- **"Must be" vs "could be"** -- re-read the question stem.

## Pacing target

~1.5 minutes per Quant question. If a question will take >2 min, **Mark** it, move on, return later.
Every question is worth the same -- bank the easy ones first.
