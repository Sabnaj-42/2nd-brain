You type a password and hit enter. What actually keeps it safe on the way to the server?

The padlock in your address bar hides 3 layers of cryptography working together — and once you see how they fit, HTTPS stops being magic.

I broke the whole thing down into 9 diagrams 👇

① Plain HTTP leaks everything — a $17 sniffer reads your password in clear text
② Symmetric encryption — fast, but how do you share the key safely?
③ Asymmetric — a public/private key pair solves the sharing problem
④ Man-in-the-middle — the one attack a key pair alone can't stop
⑤ Certificate Authorities — how your browser proves it's the real server
⑥ Session keys — where slow asymmetric hands off to fast symmetric (AES)
⑦ TLS 1.3 & forward secrecy — why a leaked key can't decrypt your past traffic

The big picture:
• CAs → prove who you're talking to
• Asymmetric → agree on a key, safely, once
• Symmetric → encrypt all the data, fast

Next time you see that padlock, you'll know exactly what's behind it.
