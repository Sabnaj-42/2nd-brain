## Text Completion (TC)

### TC-1 (single blank)
Although her early reviews were uniformly -------, the novelist's later work drew only scorn,
suggesting that her talent had declined markedly.

  (A) complimentary    (B) varied    (C) critical    (D) lukewarm    (E) anticipated

**Answer: A (complimentary)**
**Why:** "Although" signals a contrast with the later "scorn," so the early reviews must be positive.
"Complimentary" = praising. "Critical" would agree, not contrast.
**Clue:** transition word "Although" + "scorn" -> opposite direction needed.

---

### TC-2 (two blanks)
The professor's lectures were so (i) ------- that students found them (ii) -------, and attendance
soon plummeted.

  Blank (i): (A) engaging   (B) abstruse   (C) concise
  Blank (ii): (D) riveting   (E) soporific   (F) edifying

**Answer: (i) B (abstruse) + (ii) E (soporific)**
**Why:** Attendance "plummeted" -> negative outcome -> the lectures must be hard/dull and students
bored/sleepy. "abstruse" = hard to understand; "soporific" = sleep-inducing.
Check coherence: hard-to-understand lectures put students to sleep -> attendance falls. Consistent.
**Trap:** "engaging/riveting" would raise attendance, not lower it.

---

### TC-3 (single blank)
Despite the CEO's ------- pronouncements about the company's future, the quarterly report revealed
mounting losses and shrinking market share.

  (A) sanguine    (B) pessimistic    (C) equivocal    (D) ambivalent    (E) ominous

**Answer: A (sanguine)**
**Why:** "Despite" + negative reality (losses) -> the pronouncements must be positive/optimistic.
"sanguine" = optimistic. "pessimistic/ominous" would agree with the losses, not contrast them.

## Sentence Equivalence (SE)

### SE-1
The witness's testimony was so ------- that the jury could draw no reliable conclusion from it.

  (A) coherent    (B) consistent    (C) equivocal    (D) ambiguous    (E) detailed    (F) lucid

**Answer: C (equivocal) and D (ambiguous)**
**Why:** "could draw no reliable conclusion" -> the testimony must be unclear/open to interpretation.
"equivocal" and "ambiguous" are synonyms meaning open to multiple interpretations.
**Trap:** "coherent"/"lucid"/"detailed"/"consistent" would HELP the jury conclude. The pair must keep
the sentence meaning identical -- C and D both do.

---

### SE-2
The critic's review was notably -------, avoiding both lavish praise and harsh condemnation.

  (A) vehement   (B) measured   (C) equivocal   (D) temperate   (E) scathing   (F) impartial

**Answer: B (measured) and D (temperate)**
**Why:** Avoiding both extremes -> moderate/restrained tone. "measured" and "temperate" both mean
moderate/restrained, and both fit the sentence identically.
**Trap:** "impartial" (F) means unbiased -- close, but not the same meaning as moderate, and it
doesn't pair with another option to preserve meaning. Stick to the true synonym pair.

## Reading Comprehension (RC)

### RC-1
(Short passage)
"Most historians attribute the rapid industrialization of Country X in the late nineteenth century
to its abundant coal reserves. Yet neighboring Country Y, endowed with equally vast coal deposits,
industrialized far more slowly during the same period. A more convincing explanation emphasizes
Country X's early investment in universal primary education, which produced a literate workforce
able to operate and maintain complex machinery."

**Question (MC1):** The passage is primarily concerned with

  (A) comparing the educational systems of Country X and Country Y
  (B) questioning one explanation of Country X's industrialization and proposing another
  (C) arguing that coal reserves were irrelevant to industrialization
  (D) defending the traditional view of nineteenth-century industrialization
  (E) describing how machinery was maintained in Country X

**Answer: B**
**Why:** The author first challenges the coal-based explanation (using Country Y as a counterexample),
then offers the education explanation. That matches (B).
**Why others are wrong:**
- (A) too narrow / the passage never details both systems.
- (C) overstates -- coal isn't "irrelevant," just insufficient alone.
- (D) opposite -- the author challenges the traditional view.
- (E) a minor detail, not the main concern.

**Takeaway:** "primary purpose" answers must cover the WHOLE passage; eliminate narrower/overstated options.

---

### RC-2 (select-in-passage style -> here as MC1)
Same passage.

**Question:** Which of the following, if true, would most strengthen the author's preferred explanation?

  (A) Country X's coal reserves were of lower quality than Country Y's.
  (B) In both countries, literacy rates rose before industrial production did.
  (C) Country Y adopted universal primary education only in the twentieth century.
  (D) Industrial machinery of the period required skilled operators.
  (E) Country X imported most of its machinery from Country Y.

**Answer: C**
**Why:** The author claims education (not coal) drove Country X's faster industrialization. If Country
Y -- which had coal but industrialized slowly -- only adopted mass education later, that supports the
link between education and industrialization speed.
**Why D is close but weaker:** D supports that skilled operators matter but doesn't tie the
*education* variable to the *difference* between X and Y as directly as C does.
